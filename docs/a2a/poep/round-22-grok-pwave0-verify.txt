### 1. CIRCULARITY / OVERFIT — attack holds

**Gate is partly physics, partly post-hoc patch.**

| Feature | Physics motivation | Fit smell |
|---|---|---|
| rise 2–16, jerk≤0.60, hf≤0.50, sign_changes≤3 | Real (step edges, noise, non-instant rise) | Low — kills step/pulse/random without tuning narrative |
| overshoot≥0.03 | Underdamped claim | Medium — round, but model *forces* overshoot (ζ∈[0.28,0.5]) |
| **tail_slope≥−0.030** | “Damped settles” | **High — added after triangle FAR 0.58** |

Reproduced: **without** `tail_slope`, triangle FAR ≈ **0.585**, human FRR unchanged. Triangle per-condition rates: overshoot/jerk/hf ~pass; **tail alone → 0.00**. So triangle “PASS” is 100% that one threshold.

Is settle-vs-descend “real physics”? **Yes as a description of underdamped step response to a held force.** **No as a free fact about grip/IMU reflexes.** A triangle is not “anti-physics”; return-to-baseline is equally physical (stretch reflex + relaxation). You added a feature that kills *your* triangle generator, not a theorem that macros cannot look human.

**Smarter macros that beat the gate (constructed, not in the suite):**

| Shape | FAR (n=400) | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| **decayed-exp → raised plateau** | **0.965** | Trivial pass — not a reflex, just smooth rise + settle |
| **smart triangle that settles** (smoothstep peak → exp to plateau) | **1.00** | One line of “learn the gate” |
| **high-ζ 2nd-order** (same family, less ring) | **0.745** | Same model family, different params |
| **ramp-then-hold** (+ small overshoot bump) | **0.362** | Non-reflex; still >> FAR_BAR 0.05 |
| noisy-plateau | 0.105 | Borderline |
| double-step / pure 1st-order / pure sigmoid | ~0 | Gate still kills *naive* edges |

**Does this void the PASS?**  
**Yes for any strong claim** (“shape separates macros”).  
**No for a weak claim** (“shape separates *these four canned templates* under *settle-to-plateau*”).  

Harness proves: **naive step/pulse/linear-triangle/random ≠ textbook underdamped step.** It does **not** prove shape resists an adversary who (a) settles to a plateau, or (b) copies the human generator. FLIP-B is correctly out of scope; **in-scope adversary that settles is not.**

`tail_slope` threshold −0.030 sits between human mean ~0 and triangle mean ~−0.05 — classic “split the one failure.” Round-number dress does not make it pre-specified.

### 4. Correctness / report bugs

1. **`hash(cls)` in `class_pass_rate`** — `seed ^ hash(cls) & 0xFFFFFFFF` uses Python’s **salted** `str` hash. FRR/FAR **not stable across processes**; doc’s 0.012 vs live 0.015/0.025. `test_deterministic_under_seed` only same-process. **Fix:** `zlib.adler32(cls.encode())` or fixed map — not `hash()`.

2. **`rise_samples`:** `range(pidx + 1)` is `0..pidx` — OK. No div-by-zero: `amp or 1e-9`, `e1 or 1e-9`. Flat line fails closed. **No crash bugs.**

3. **`overshoot_ratio` mis-semantics:** triangle mean overshoot **0.73** is not “overshot then settled”; it is **peak high + tail still low (descending)**. Feature double-counts settle with `tail_slope` and **would also fire on return-to-baseline “humans.”** Label/claim oversells “underdamped overshoot.”

4. **`rise_decay_asym` computed, never gated** — dead; fine, not a claim bug.

5. **Report claim string** correctly says synthetic / not HIL / not FLIP-B; **does not** condition on settle-to-plateau (code comment does; `separability_report()["claim"]` does not).

6. No test that a **settling non-human** fails — only that triangle’s tail is more negative. Circularity untested by design.
